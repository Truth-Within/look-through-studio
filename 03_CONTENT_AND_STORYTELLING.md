# Editorial and Storytelling Guide — The Human Question

## Channel voice
Calm, intelligent, curious, slightly eerie where appropriate; conversational like a thoughtful friend explaining something mind-blowing late at night. Human, reflective, emotionally engaging, never robotic or pretentiously academic.

## Topics
Human evolution, psychology, behavior, belief, biology, science, history, ancient civilizations, philosophy, everyday mysteries, technology, and how humans live, think, and act.

## Narrative arc
Hook -> surprising observation -> mystery -> personal relevance -> evidence and explanation -> deeper question -> strongest insight -> satisfying, thought-provoking conclusion.

## Scriptwriting
Start in a concrete moment or with a striking question, paradox, or grounded surprising fact. Early sentences should be short and clear. Vary sentence length naturally. Use analogies, specific examples, occasional self-correction, curiosity loops, rhetorical questions, and pacing. Avoid generic intros ('in this video'), jargon, formulaic filler, and forced engagement requests. Make the ending reframe the topic. Do not fabricate named researchers, dates, studies, or numbers. Mark uncertain or disputed claims clearly.

## Long-form presets
A: 5–6 minutes (approximately 800–1000 words)
B: 8–10 minutes (approximately 1300–1600 words)
C: 12–15 minutes (approximately 2000–2500 words)
These are editorial targets; actual spoken length and audio duration determine scene timing.

## Visual rules
Stickmen: clean black lines, round heads, expressive features. White background for simple scenes; contextual environments for historical or dramatic moments. Flat props with bold outlines. Red for warnings/emphasis, blue for neutral objects, yellow for warm/paper elements, brown for ancient/earth themes. Use diagrams, comparisons, labeled statistics, and timelines. Every visual should explain or embody the current spoken idea. Avoid generic or unrelated images.

## Pacing
The original creative prompt targets approximately 27 images/minute and suggested 148, 243, or 365 images depending on length. Treat these as historical reference, NOT hard rendering requirements. Prefer fluid animation with scene changes when narratively justified. Occasional visual or rhetorical pattern interrupts may help, but do not force them mechanically.

## Narration-to-visual traceability
Each scene stores exact source script text and accurate timestamps; no paraphrased 'voiceover' field. Use word-level or sentence-level alignment as available. Character actions and infographics must represent the spoken facts accurately.

## Metadata
Generate high-contrast, uncluttered thumbnails; descriptive titles; compelling descriptions; a source section with genuine, verifiable references; and relevant hashtags. No misleading claims or invented citations.
